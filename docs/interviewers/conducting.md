# Conducting the interview

Let them teach you, then drill down. The structure is fixed so every candidate gets the same chance; the questions follow the candidate's own work, so rehearsed answers run out quickly.

## The standard 75-minute technical interview

| Minutes | Block | Lead | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Open | Hiring lead | Introductions, format, "we will probe in depth; 'I don't know' is fine" |
| 5–30 | Deep-dive on real work | Hiring lead | Depth, ownership, communication |
| 30–45 | What-if challenge | Independent | Reasoning, failure handling, judgement |
| 45–60 | Role scenario | Either | The same project-shaped problem for every candidate |
| 60–70 | Coding check | Either | Clarify, implement and test a simple requirement |
| 70–75 | Their questions | Hiring lead | Curiosity; sell the role |

## The deep-dive ladder
1. **Problem:** what was hard, and for whom?
2. **Ownership:** what did you own, and what did others own?
3. **Mechanics:** how does it work? Where is it stored? What happens next?
4. **Quality:** how did you test and monitor it?
5. **Outcome:** what changed, and what would you redo?

## What-if prompts
- "What if this message arrives twice?"
- "What if that dependency is down for an hour?"
- "What if traffic grows ten times?"
- "What if the rule changes after go-live?"
- "Which option would you choose, and what would change your mind?"

## Opening script
> Thanks for joining. I'm [name], [role] on [project]; [co-interviewer] is from another team and brings an independent view. We'll spend most of the time on something you've built, then change a few constraints, then a short problem and a small coding task. We'll ask follow-up questions to understand your thinking; saying "I don't know" is fine. You'll have time for your questions at the end.

## Behavioural questions (STAR)
Ask for one real situation, then probe the **S**ituation, the **T**ask, the candidate's own **A**ctions and the **R**esult. Follow up with "what did *you* do?" and "what would you do differently?" Hypothetical answers ("I would...") are weaker evidence than what they did.

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Allow silence and thinking time | Make one obscure framework detail the deciding question |
| Give one hint, and note that you did | Show your verdict during the interview |
| Take notes close to word for word | Discuss the candidate before submitting your form |
| Keep to time; move on politely | Ask about age, family, religion, health or other personal life |
| Be candid about deadlines, pace and client exposure | Interview on a project you have not been briefed on |

## Difficult moments

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The candidate is stuck | Narrow the question, give one hint, then move on. Score what they did with the hint. |
| The candidate is very nervous | Start with their own project, slow down, return to a missed point later. |
| Answers sound rehearsed | Change one constraint on their own example and ask them to adapt it. |
| Suspected hidden AI or outside help in a live session | Ask them to explain the last step in their own words, then extend it. Record what you observed; do not accuse. |
| Remote connection problems | Reschedule rather than score a broken session. Camera on; shared editor for coding. |
