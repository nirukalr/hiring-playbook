# Architect

Shapes solutions across projects, owns technical risk, and influences without direct authority.

**Candidate time:** about 6 hours · **Decision:** within 35 days

## What we look for
- Architecture choices explained with trade-offs, cost and risk
- Security, data, integration and migration thinking, not only diagrams
- Has seen designs through to production and lived with the result
- Brings tech leads and clients along through influence

**Must-haves:** technical depth; system design and architecture; leadership and influence.

## Panel
Two members of the Architects' Forum (one independent of the project), the hiring lead, two tech leads, and the CTO for the final conversation.

## Stages
| Stage | Format | Time | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture portfolio presentation | One or two real, sanitised architectures: context, options, decision, outcome, regrets | 60 min | Architects' Forum × 2 |
| Architecture kata | A realistic brief with constraints (tenancy, compliance, cost, legacy); the panel changes a constraint midway | 90 min | Architects' Forum + hiring lead |
| POC or design review (optional) | Time-boxed POC (2–3 days) on the project's riskiest technology, or a written review of an existing design | Varies | Architects' Forum |
| Stakeholder session | With tech leads and a delivery head: how they would work with teams and clients | 60 min | Tech leads + delivery head |
| HR culture interview | Structured values interview | 45 min | HR |
| CTO conversation | Vision, technical strategy, expectations | 45 min | CTO |

## Sample questions
- "Which architecture decision do you most regret? What signal did you miss?"
- "Now the client needs data residency in two regions. What changes?"
- "How do you stop a team from adopting a technology you think is wrong?"
- "How do you estimate the run cost of this design?"

## Red flags
- Diagrams without failure modes, cost or migration
- Never stayed long enough to live with their designs
- Wins arguments by authority rather than evidence
