# Senior Software Engineer

About 5 or more years. Owns complex features and technical decisions in their area, and mentors others.

**Candidate time:** about 5 hours · **Decision:** within 21 days (28 with a case study)

## What we look for
- Explains production systems they owned, including failures and trade-offs
- Designs for idempotency, consistency, observability and safe release
- Clarifies requirements with product owners and clients
- Raises the quality of the people around them

**Must-haves:** technical depth; system design and architecture; ownership and delivery.

## Panel
Project tech lead plus an independent SSE, Tech Lead or Architect matched on discipline. Two project engineers for the team session.

## Stages
| Stage | Format | Time | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical interview | Standard 75-min structure; deep-dive goes into production behaviour | 75 min | Hiring lead + independent |
| System design | A project-shaped problem, for example a claims workflow API with queues. Probe data model, failure, scale, cost, rollout | 60 min | Independent + project engineer |
| Case study or POC (only for a named doubt) | Core case study (1–2 days), Full (3–5 days) or POC (1–2 days), then a 60-min walkthrough with a live change | Varies | Hiring lead + second reviewer |
| Team session | Review a realistic pull request with planted problems, then open Q&A with the team | 60 min | Project engineers |
| HR culture interview | Structured values interview | 45 min | HR |

## Sample questions
- "What was the worst production incident in something you owned? What changed afterwards?"
- "The database write succeeds but publishing the event fails. What now?"
- "Which practices are non-negotiable for you, and why?"
- "How have you helped a junior engineer get better?"

## Red flags
- Stays at tool names; stalls on "how?"
- Defends the first design against new facts
- No examples of influencing quality beyond their own code
